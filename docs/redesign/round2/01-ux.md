# Round 2: the owner's walk, and what changes

Role: principal UX, same as last round.
Build: this branch with the calibration and clock-rate work in progress, dev server, Chromium with fake capture, 19 Nov 2026.
Walk: as the owner did. Baritone voice, difficulty 1, speakers, no test clock, calibration skipped (the fake microphone cannot hear clicks). A second walk used the test clock, wired headphones and a synthetic baritone that scoops into each note and lets the voice trail down at the end of it, to see what a real singer's tails do to the marks.
Screenshots: `docs/redesign/round2/shots/`. `d-` is desktop 1440 x 900, `p-` is phone 390 x 844, `t-` is the trailing-voice take.

Everything below is a decision. Where a decision crosses an earlier ruling, it says so and names the ruling it asks the owner to replace.
Updated after the design critique (`05-critique.md`): sections 0, 2, 3, 4, 5.3, 10, 11, 12, 14 and 15 carry the settled disagreements D1 to D7.

## 0. Summary of decisions

1. The visual language stays. No restyling this round.
2. Picker: a "Play my range" button and the four note chips play in the instrument's own sound (sampled; voice is a sung "ah" per voice type). Nothing plays by itself.
3. A new "Match a note" warm-up: one note, a live needle, "You hit it". Not graded. It is a step in onboarding after calibration, and a permanent entry on Practice. It sits outside the 1 to 10 scale.
4. The calibration banner is removed. A quiet calibration status line lives on Practice and the read-through. It only turns to a warning when the setup changed since the last calibration.
5. "Take" becomes "try" everywhere the player reads. The streak rule reads "3 ranked tries". Results and Practice link to previous tries; the try detail shows the marks, plays the music, and plays back what the app heard.
6. Practice right panel: no inner scrolling at 1440 x 900, one screen at 390 x 844. Key and time signature become compact chip rows; tempo and length share a row; XP moves to one line.
7. The key note says what is true: "Difficulty 1 uses major keys with up to 1 sharp or flat. Minor keys start at difficulty 2."
8. "Time" becomes "Time signature".
9. A failed clock-rate measurement never blocks a try. Error screens name the cause in plain words and offer "Send diagnostics" (section 15.4).
10. The starting pitch before the count-in gets its own clearly labelled moment, with no microphone marks during it. The count-in shows which bar it is in, and the last bar says "Come in after 4". Count-in length stays as configured: 2 bars at difficulties 1 to 3 (05 D1).
11. Practice mode has no reading timer. "Reading time's up. Start when ready." is deleted.
12. The "New: hear the tempo..." coach mark is deleted.
13. "Before you play" loses its tick boxes and its self-ticking. It becomes "Look before you play": five things to spot, no boxes.
14. At difficulties 1 to 3, the live screen shows only green and red on written notes, and only when the app is sure. No other live mark (05 D5). Purple ghost and extra marks wait for the results. Trailing pitch at the end of a note is folded into that note. Each try gets a "How sure we are" line, and results get "Send diagnostics" (05 D3).

## 2. Hearing the instrument in the picker

What he hit: `d-01-picker-baritone.png`. The selection panel has four note chips (G2, C3, C4, G4). They play, but in one soft piano-like tone for every instrument (`src/platform/web/audio/listen-voice.ts`: three additive partials, "one soft piano-like tone for every instrument"). A baritone hears a piano, so the sound does not tell him "this is my voice".

Why: the Listen voice was built as a neutral reference (05 section 9.2). The picker reused it.

Fix:

- **What plays.** The four chips stay, in this order: lowest of the full range, low end of the served range at the shown difficulty, high end of the served range, highest of the full range. A new button above them plays all four in order, rising: "Play my range". Each note 0.8 s with 0.15 s between. While a note sounds, its chip and its notehead on the staff are brass, and the keyboard key under it is pressed.
- **Timbre.** Each instrument plays its own sampled sound (the sound researcher's samples, one sample set per instrument family member, pitch-shifted at most 3 semitones). Voices sing an open "ah", synthesised per voice type (`02-sounds.md`: no licensed solo-voice samples exist). The owner hears the formant voice beside the choir set before build and his choice goes in PLAN. The choir set is never used for the starting pitch or Match a note: its ensemble detuning is as wide as the in-tune band. Same samples are used everywhere a single note or chord plays (picker, Practice range card, "Hear starting pitch", "Match a note", Listen). The metronome does not change.
- **Stand-in.** An instrument without its own set plays the nearest one (euphonium plays trombone, `02-sounds.md`) and the panel says so, with no promise of a future sound.
- **Switching cards** while a sound plays stops it.
- **Never by itself.** Choosing a card plays nothing. Sound only starts on a press.
- **Picker before microphone.** The picker only plays. It does not listen (the microphone has not been asked for yet). Matching comes later (section 3).
- **States.** Playing: the button reads "Stop" with the stop glyph. Audio blocked: the caption line reads `picker.play.failed`.

## 3. "Match a note": an even simpler start

What he hit: difficulty 1 is already real music: 8 bars of Schubert in F major at 60 (`d-07-scan.png`). There is nothing simpler.

Why: the app has no step that asks for one note and only one.

Decision: a warm-up called "Match a note". It is not difficulty 0. Difficulty stays 1 to 10 and keeps meaning "how hard the music is". Match a note has no score, no XP, no rank, no streak, and nothing goes to History.

### 3.1 What it is

One note on a staff in the player's clef. The app plays it, the player sings or plays it, a needle shows flat or sharp, and the screen says when they hit it.

- **The note.** Chosen from the served range at difficulty 1 for the instrument (baritone: C3 to C4), from the tonic chord of C major first, then any natural note. The first note is always the middle of that range, rounded to a chord tone (baritone: E3 or G3). The player can tap any chip of the range card instead to choose the note.
- **Hearing it.** The note plays 1.5 s in the instrument's sound (section 2) when the screen opens and on "Play it again". It is the same tone path as the starting-pitch reference (`voiceReferenceTones` first-note part), so the warm-up teaches exactly what "Hear starting pitch" will sound like in a try.
- **Listening.** The microphone opens only after the note has finished sounding, plus 300 ms, so the app never hears its own note through speakers.
- **The needle.** A horizontal meter, flat on the left, sharp on the right, ±50 cents. The middle band is the in-tune window at difficulty 1 from `config/grading-config.json` (voice ±25 cents today). The needle moves only while a steady pitch is heard. Above it, the note the player is producing in letters ("You: F3").
- **Voice octaves.** Voice is octave-free, as in tries (R7): a baritone singing E2 against E3 hits it, and the screen says "An octave lower. That counts." Instruments must match the octave; a wrong octave says "Right note, wrong octave."
- **Direction.** Flat and sharp say which way to go in words, not only with a chevron (`match.flat`, `match.sharp`).
- **A hit.** In the band for 0.8 s in a row. The note turns green with a tick, the line reads `match.hit`, and after 1.5 s the next note comes. A note chosen from the range chips stays until another is chosen. The onboarding step never moves on by itself. The octave lines show only when the sung octave is inside the voice's full range. Five notes in a round, then a one-line summary and "Five more" or "Done".
- **Not heard.** After 6 s with no steady pitch: `match.notHeard`.
- **Too far.** More than 3 semitones away for 2 s: `match.far` with an arrow saying up or down.

### 3.2 Where it lives

1. **Onboarding.** A new step after calibration and before difficulty: "Sing one note" (voice) or "Play one note" (instruments). One note only, not a round of five. It doubles as the microphone check: if the player hits it, the step says so and offers Continue; if the note was far from the range in a way that suggests another voice type (a baritone card, but every attempt sits above the range), it says `match.onboarding.otherVoice` with a link back to the picker. Skip is always there. Steps become 6.
2. **The picker.** The picker plays only (section 2). It does not host matching, because the microphone is not allowed yet.
3. **Practice, permanently.** Desktop: a full-width secondary button at the bottom of the left instrument card, under the range lines: "Match a note". Phone: a row under the summary card: "Warm up: match a note" with a chevron. It is not a difficulty in the stepper.
4. **Read-through.** Not offered. The read-through has "Hear starting pitch".

Route: `#/match`, focused chrome (back arrow, title, no tabs). Without the microphone, the screen shows `match.noMic` and "Allow microphone".

### 3.3 Relation to difficulty 1

Difficulty 1 description stays "Steps and small leaps in quarters and halves". Under the stepper at difficulty 1 only, one line: `setup.difficulty.matchHint` with a link to the warm-up. Nothing about the warm-up changes the player's difficulty or placement.

## 4. Calibration status that stays put

What he hit: `d-05-practice.png`. "Calibrate once so timing grades are fair. It takes 15 seconds." on every visit to Practice, plus, below in the Mode control, "Ranked needs a calibrated setup on this device." with a second "Calibrate now" button. After the try, results showed "Your audio delay changed. Recalibrate for fair timing." although he was never calibrated (`d-11-results.png`).

Why it keeps coming back (from the code):

1. **A calibration is saved only when it passes.** `CalibrationCard.tsx` saves only `outcome.accepted`. On his Mac the clock-rate check failed falsely (the engineers' fix), so no calibration was stored. The banner's test (`app-services.ts`, `hasCalibration`: any stored calibration that is not `reported`) stays false.
2. **Closing the banner only lasts until the next page load.** `dismissed` is an in-memory value, never written to storage. Every reload brings it back.
3. **The banner and Ranked disagree.** The banner accepts a calibration for any output. Ranked requires a measured, non-stale calibration for the current route key. A Mac whose output changes (display speakers, AirPods, back to built-in) makes the calibration stale and Ranked blocked, while the banner stays quiet. Or the reverse.
4. **Results warn about a delay change with nothing to compare.** The count-in recheck shift line shows for a player with no measured calibration.

Fix:

- **Delete the banner** (`banner.calibrateNeeded`) and its dismiss state. The mode hint already says why Ranked is unavailable.
- **One status line, one source of truth.** Status comes from the same predicate as Ranked availability (`calibrationForRoute` on the current route), so the two can never disagree.
- **Where.** Practice desktop: right end of the line under the page title, replacing the space the banner took, beside the streak line. Practice phone: the last line inside the summary card. Read-through: desktop, a line under "Start playing", above Sounds, reading "Not calibrated · Calibrate" (no "tries count as practice": an uncalibrated player cannot be in Ranked); phone, one line under the meta line, always visible, and also the first item of the overflow menu. Settings > Sound keeps its full line.
- **Press it.** Opens a popover with the detail line and "Calibrate again" (or "Calibrate"). On the read-through the popover says calibrating will leave this music and bring it back after (the excerpt is kept, not counted as seen again).

States and copy:

When it may nag, and when it must not:

- **Never** a banner, toast or modal about calibration on Practice, the read-through or results, except the two cases below.
- **Caution colour** only for "Changed". That is the only time the player's earlier work is now wrong.
- **Results** say once, under the score, `results.calibration.uncalibrated` (the existing "Not calibrated: timing marks may be a little off") when the try was not calibrated. Nothing else.
- **Recheck shift** (`results.banner.recheckShift`) shows only when the try had a measured calibration and the count-in measured a shift beyond the config threshold. Never for an uncalibrated try.
- **Ranked blocked** keeps `setup.mode.blocked.calibration`, but the separate "Calibrate now" button below it goes; the word "Calibrate" inside the line is the link: `setup.mode.blocked.calibration` becomes "Ranked needs calibration on this device. {link}" with `{link}` = `calStatus.action.calibrate`.

### 5.3 Previous tries

What he hit: results have no way to the previous tries (`d-11`, `t-04`). History exists (`d-12`) and its detail shows the marked score with Listen (`t-05`), but nothing points to it, and the not-graded row shows a bare "–".

Decision:

- **Results.** Under the action row, one line: "{n} tries at this piece · See all tries" when this piece has earlier tries on this device, else "See all tries". "See all tries" opens History. "{n} tries at this piece" opens History filtered to this piece. Never "Try {n} at this piece", which reads as an instruction.
- **Tries today.** One count everywhere (Practice header, History day group, streak line): every try started today that reached bar 1, graded or not.
- **Practice.** Desktop: under the "Start" button, one quiet line: "Last try: {grade} {score}, {title} · All tries". Phone: the same line under Start, shortened to "Last try: {grade} {score} · All tries". Absent before the first try.
- **History list.** Each row shows the grade letter and score at the left (or "Not graded" in words, not "–"), then title, then the summary line, then Ranked or Practice. A filter "This piece" appears when opened from results.
- **Try detail.** Shows, top to bottom: title and summary; "Try 2 of 3 at this piece" with previous and next arrows when there are several; the marked score with "My marks / Clean score"; three buttons: "Listen" (the written music, as now), "Hear what we heard" (new, playback glyph, not the microphone), "Send diagnostics" (section 15); the score breakdown.
- **Hear what we heard.** Plays the try's detected notes (`AttemptRecord.detected`: pitch, onset, length) in the instrument's sound, with the bar highlight following. No audio is stored (R9 stands); this is a re-synthesis of what the app heard, which is also the clearest way for a player to understand an unexpected mark. While it plays, the caption reads "What we heard, bar {n} of {total}".

Copy:

## 15. Marks the player did not mean, and diagnostics

What he hit: during the exercise, many marks he did not intend; he suspects his voice trailing at the ends of notes was heard as new notes.

What the walk shows: the synthetic baritone with a scoop into each note and a 1.6-semitone fall over 220 ms at each note's end (`test-results/round2/trail.ts`). Live, a violet ghost B♭ and a violet "+" appeared in the rest of bar 4, right where the voice trailed off the half note (`t-03`). Results kept 2 extra notes in rests (`t-04`: "2 extra", two violet ghosts with "+"). With green dots on every right note and violet on the tails, the eye goes to the violet. A real voice in a real room, with vibrato, breath and speaker bleed, will produce more than this clean synthetic one.

Why: the follower treats a pitch change with enough level as a possible note. A tail that leaves the written note's time, or falls into a rest, has no written note to attach to, so it becomes an extra. Live, the screen draws it at once. R11 forbids weakening live feedback, and R60 kept `src/follow` closed last round.

Decisions (this asks the owner to amend R11 for difficulties 1 to 3, and to open `src/follow` and `src/dsp` for the tolerance below):

### 15.1 Fewer, calmer marks at low difficulties

- **Difficulties 1 to 3, live:** only red on written notes (wrong, missed, early, late, out of tune) and the green right-note dot, each only when the follower is sure. When it is not sure, nothing is drawn: no grey ring or other "unsure" mark (05 D5). No violet ghost notes and no extra "+" during the try. They appear on the results score.
- **Difficulty 4 and up:** as now.
- **"After the try" setting** still hides everything live, at every difficulty.
- **Setting** Display > "Show played notes live" with values "From difficulty 4" (default), "Always", "Never". Hint `settings.display.ghostsLive.hint`.

### 15.2 Tolerance for tails and scoops (follower)

- **Tail.** A pitch movement with no new attack, starting after 60% of a note's written length or after its end, moving continuously away (glide, not a step), with level falling, is part of that note. It never creates an extra, a ghost or a wrong pitch. Its time does not lengthen the note's duration for rhythm.
- **Scoop.** A glide into a note in its first 120 ms (voice) is part of the onset; intonation is measured after it (the existing steady-pitch rule).
- **Extras need conviction.** An extra note at difficulties 1 to 3 needs a fresh attack and at least 200 ms of steady pitch (voice and strings), 120 ms (others), and a level no more than 12 dB under the try's median note level. Quieter or shorter events are logged in diagnostics, not marked.
- Values live in `config/grading-config.json` with the musician's other tolerances and pass the floor check.

### 15.3 "How sure we are", per try

Under the score breakdown on results and try detail, one line, from data the try already has: the share of notes whose frames agree (`DetectedNote` frames-agree flag), the number of marks changed after the try, the noise floor and the input level.

| Level | Rule (config) | Key | Text |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clear | ≥ 90% agree, ≤ 2 changed, input level fine | `sure.clear` | We heard you clearly. |
| Mostly | 75 to 90% agree, or 3 to 5 changed | `sure.mostly` | We heard most of it. {n, plural, one {# mark} other {# marks}} may be wrong. |
| Unsure | below that | `sure.unsure` | We weren't sure about many notes. Treat these marks as a rough guide. |
| Help | Unsure only | `sure.unsure.help` | Move the mic away from the speakers, or use headphones, and sing a little louder. |

On an Unsure try at difficulties 1 to 3, the results score shows extras and ghosts at 50% opacity (the existing `braked-ghost-opacity` treatment) so the reliable red marks lead.

### 15.4 Diagnostics per try

- **Results and try detail:** a tertiary button "Send diagnostics" under the "How sure we are" line, after the prompt `diagnostics.prompt`. Also on every error screen and the calibration result.
- **What it does:** opens a dialog (sheet on phone) that says what is in the file and where to send it (`diagnostics.sendTo`, from a configured support address), then "Save file" and, where the browser can share files, "Share…". No backend (R9). While no support address is configured, the button and title read "Save diagnostics" and the send line is absent (05 D3).
- **File name:** `sightread-try-{yyyy-mm-dd}-{hhmm}.json`. `src/session/diagnostics.ts` uses the same name.

- **What is in it:** app, core, config and corpus versions; browser and platform; instrument, difficulty, settings resolved; the piece id and transposition; calibration (method, delay, spread, when, output, stale, clock-rate error); the count-in recheck; `audioEnv` with gate failures; input level and noise floor over the try; the expected notes; every detected note with onset, length, pitch, cents, frames-agree flag and level; every live mark with the time it was drawn and every change made after the try; extras, including those below the 15.2 threshold that were not drawn; a pitch-and-level track every 10 ms for the whole try (numbers only, no sound); errors and warnings logged during the try. The track is kept in memory for the latest try and with the stored try only while it is the most recent ten; older tries export without it.
