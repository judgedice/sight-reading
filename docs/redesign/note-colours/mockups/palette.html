Note colours: the palette

Twelve colours, in the classroom order that runs up from C: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, pink. The seven letter colours do the work on the page: a sharp or flat keeps its letter's colour, and the chip says F♯ or B♭. The five in-between colours are pale on purpose, because at full strength they land on the feedback hues; they are used only on a coloured keyboard. Every chip carries its letter. Closest letter colour to error, correct or ghost (ΔE2000, normal vision): light fills 18.7 (C), dark fills 18.4 (C), light edges 12.2 (A).

Close pairs are almost all on the red-to-green run (C, D, E, F), which collapses to yellows and browns for the 1 in 12 men with a red-green deficiency. They stay apart in lightness where they can (E and F differ by 0.26 in luminance), and the letter on the chip always separates them. Under deuteranopia and protanopia the light edges of F and A come within 3 to 5 of error and ghost: that is why colour never shares a score with marks in option B.
